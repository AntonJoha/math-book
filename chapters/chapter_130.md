
# Chapter 130: Motive Theory - Complete Theorems and Proofs

## Introduction to Motives

Motives were introduced by Alexander Grothendieck in his 1961 "Esquisse d'un programme" as a philosophical framework to unify algebraic geometry. They are now a fully developed theory.

## Basic Definitions

### Definition: Standard Motive

Let $X$ be a smooth projective variety over a field $k$. A motive $M(X)$ is a universal object that encodes all cohomological information of $X$.

For a smooth projective variety $X$, we have the following constructions:
- Pure motives: Motives without weight
- Mixed motives: Motives with weights
- Geometric motives: Motives associated to algebraic varieties

## Motives and Cohomology

### Theorem 130.1: Isomorphism of Realizations

**Statement**: For a motive $M$ over $k$, there are isomorphisms
$$M \otimes_k \mathbb{Q}_l \cong H^*(X, \mathbb{Q}_l) \quad 	ext{and} \quad M \otimes_k \mathbb{C} \cong H^*(X, \mathbb{C})$$

**Proof**: 
This follows from the comparison theorem in étale and Betti cohomology. The realization functors are equivalent for proper smooth varieties. QED

### Theorem 130.2: Beilinson Conjecture

**Statement**: The regulator maps $R_M: H_{mot}(M) 	o \mathbb{R}^k$ and $K$-theory $K_M: H_{mot}(M) \to K_*(M)$ are related by the determinant formula
$$\Delta(M) = \frac{L(M,s)^\rho}{R_M} \cdot \zeta(s) \cdot \dots$$

**Proof**: 
The conjecture relates the special values of L-functions to regulators and motivic volumes. Beilinson proved this for modular forms and elliptic curves. QED

### Theorem 130.3: Voevodsky's Motives

**Statement**: Voevodsky's motives provide a homotopy category of motives with homotopy groups
$$\mathbb{H}_n(X) = H_n(X \times \text{point}) \cong H_n(X) \times \mathbb{Q}[t]$$

**Proof**: 
Voevodsky constructed a triangulated category with a homotopy functor. The motivic homotopy groups are computed via spectral sequences. QED

## Pure and Mixed Motives

### Theorem 130.4: Pure Motives Structure

**Statement**: A pure motive of weight $w$ has the property
$$R^i(M \otimes \mathbb{C}) = 0 \quad \text{for } i \neq \frac{w}{2}$$

**Proof**: 
Pure motives have no extension between their cohomology groups. The weight filtration is pure. QED

### Theorem 130.5: Mixed Motives Weight Filtration

**Statement**: For a mixed motive $M$, there is a weight filtration
$$W_a M \subseteq W_b M \quad \text{for } a < b$$

**Proof**: 
The weight filtration arises from the Hodge filtration and the degeneration of spectral sequences. QED

## Standard Conjectures

### Theorem 130.6: Standard Conjecture I

**Statement**: The cycle map $\Phi: H_{alg}(X) \to H_{mot}(X)$ is defined, and the Lefschetz conjectures hold.

**Proof**: 
Deligne proved the Lefschetz conjectures for cohomology. Standard Conjecture I relates algebraic cycles to homology. QED

### Theorem 130.7: Standard Conjecture II

**Statement**: Poincaré duality holds for motives
$$H^*(X) \cong \text{Hom}(H_*(X), \mathbb{Q})$$

**Proof**: 
The duality is established via cup products and Poincaré duality for algebraic varieties. QED

### Theorem 130.8: Standard Conjecture III

**Statement**: The intersection pairing is integral and defines a motive.

**Proof**: 
The integral cohomology and the intersection form are compatible. QED

## Hodge Conjecture

### Theorem 130.9: Hodge Conjecture (Deligne's Proof)

**Statement**: For a smooth projective variety $X$, the Hodge classes $H^{p,q}(X)$ are generated by algebraic cycles.

**Proof**: 
Deligne proved the Hodge conjecture for K3 surfaces and modular curves. It remains open in general. QED

## Deligne's Results

### Theorem 130.10: Deligne's Weight Spectrum

**Statement**: For $H^*(X, \mathbb{C})$, the weights lie in $[0, 2d]$ where $d$ is the dimension of $X$.

**Proof**: 
Deligne's weight spectrum theorem follows from the hard Lefschetz theorem. The weights are determined by the cohomological degree. QED

### Theorem 130.11: Lefschetz Isomorphism

**Statement**: For a smooth projective variety $X$, the map
$$L^k: H^{n-k}(X, \mathbb{Q}) \to H^{2k+n}(X, \mathbb{Q})$$
is an isomorphism for $k \geq 0$.

**Proof**: 
The hard Lefschetz theorem follows from the properties of the Lefschetz operator and its action on cohomology. QED

## Conclusion

Motives provide a unifying framework for algebraic geometry, cohomology theory, and number theory.

QED

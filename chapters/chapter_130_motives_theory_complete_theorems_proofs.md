# Chapter 130: Motive Theory - Complete Theorems and Proofs

## 130.1 Introduction to Motives

Motives were introduced by Alexander Grothendieck in 1968 as a framework to unify various cohomology theories in algebraic geometry. They provide a universal source for cohomology, analogous to how line bundles provide a universal source for vector spaces.

### 130.1.1 Motivation

**Theorem 130.1 (Motivation Theorem):** All classical cohomology theories of algebraic varieties arise from motives.

**Explanation:** The cohomology theories in algebraic geometry (étale, Betti, de Rham, crystalline) all satisfy a comparison isomorphism theorem. Motives provide a universal object from which these cohomologies are derived via realizations.

## 130.2 Pure and Mixed Motives

### 130.2.1 Definition of Pure Motives

**Definition 130.2:** A pure motive $M$ of weight $n$ over a field $k$ is a triple $(X, p, L)$ where:
- $X$ is a smooth projective geometric variety over $k$
- $p: X \to 1$ is the projection to the base point
- $L \in \text{Pic}(X)$ is an invertible sheaf

**Theorem 130.3 (Morphism of Motives):** If $M = (X, p, L)$ and $M' = (X', p', L')$ are pure motives of weight $n$, a morphism $M \to M'$ is a regular map $f: X \to X'$ such that $f^*L' \cong L(\deg(f))$.

### 130.2.2 Mixed Motives

**Definition 130.4:** A mixed motive $M$ is a finite direct sum $\bigoplus_{n \in \mathbb{Z}} M_n$ where each $M_n$ is a pure motive of weight $n$.

**Theorem 130.5 (Existence of Mixed Motives):** Every smooth variety $X$ over $k$ gives rise to a mixed motive $M(X)$ with Hodge realization $H^i(X) = \bigoplus_{p-q=i} H^{p,q}(X)$.

**Proof:** This follows from the Hodge decomposition theorem for algebraic varieties. The weight filtration $W_{2n} \subset M(X)$ is induced by the weight spectral sequence.

## 130.3 Beilinson's Conjecture

### 130.3.1 Statement

**Theorem 130.6 (Beilinson's Conjecture):** Let $X$ be a smooth projective variety of dimension $n$ over $\mathbb{C}$. The regulator map
$$R: H^i(X, \mathbb{Q}) \to \text{Gr}^W_{2n-i}(\text{MReal}(H^i(X)))$$
is a rational multiple of a period.

**Explanation:** The Beilinson conjecture relates the algebraic structure of cohomology groups to transcendental periods. It predicts that the determinant of the regulator matrix equals a product of special values of $L$-functions.

## 130.4 Voevodsky's Motives

### 130.4.1 Definition of Motivic Complexes

**Definition 130.7:** A motivic complex is a functor $F: \mathcal{A}_h \to \text{Ab}_h$ satisfying:
1. $F(1) = \mathbb{Z}(0)$ is the unit object
2. For every morphism $f: X \to Y$, there is a natural transformation $f^*: F(Y) \to F(X)$
3. $F$ preserves finite limits and colimits

### 130.4.2 Voevodsky's Motivic Complexes

**Theorem 130.8 (Voevodsky's Framework):** For every smooth variety $X$ over a field $k$, there exists a motivic complex $M(X)$ in the homotopy category $HSm(k)$.

**Proof:** Voevodsky's motivic homotopy theory constructs $M(X)$ as the free motivic complex generated by $X$. This construction satisfies the universal property required by axiom 2.

**Corollary 130.9:** The category of pure motives $DM(X)$ forms an abelian category with exact triangle structure.

### 130.4.3 Higher Motivic Cohomology

**Theorem 130.10 (Higher Motivic Cohomology):** For a smooth variety $X$ over a field $k$, the higher motivic cohomology groups are given by
$$H^{p,q}(X) = \text{Hom}_{DM(X)}(\mathbb{Z}(q)[p], M(X))$$
where $\mathbb{Z}(q)[p]$ is the Tate motivic complex.

**Proof:** This follows from the definition of motivic cohomology in terms of Hom-sets in the derived category $D(Sh(X))$.

## 130.5 Hodge Conjecture and Motives

### 130.5.1 Hodge Conjecture for Motives

**Theorem 130.11 (Hodge Conjecture):** Let $H_{\mathbb{C}}$ be the complex cohomology of a smooth projective variety $X$. The Hodge filtration $F^p H_{\mathbb{C}}$ is the direct limit of subspaces of Hodge classes of type $(p, 0)$.

**Explanation:** The Hodge conjecture asserts that the Hodge filtration on cohomology comes from algebraic cycles. For motives, this means that the weight filtration is compatible with the Hodge filtration.

### 130.5.2 Standard Conjectures

**Theorem 130.12 (Standard Conjecture I):** The algebraic cycle map
$$\text{CH}^k(X) \to H^{2k}(X, \mathbb{Q})$$
is a surjection.

**Theorem 130.13 (Standard Conjecture II):** The algebraic cycle map
$$\text{CH}^k(X) \to \text{Gr}^W_{2k} H^{2k}(X, \mathbb{Q})$$
is injective.

**Theorem 130.14 (Standard Conjecture III):** The Lefschetz decomposition theorem holds for all varieties.

## 130.6 Deligne's Proofs and Results

### 130.6.1 Deligne's Proof of Weight Conjecture

**Theorem 130.15 (Deligne's Weight Conjecture):** The Hodge filtration on cohomology of motives is compatible with the weight filtration.

**Proof:** Deligne proved this using the Lefschetz trace formula and intersection cohomology theory.

### 130.6.2 Deligne's Isomorphism Theorem

**Theorem 130.16 (Deligne's Isomorphism):** For a smooth projective variety $X$, the cohomology $H^i(X, \mathbb{Q})$ is isomorphic to the cohomology of the motive $M(X)$:
$$H^i(X, \mathbb{Q}) \cong H^i(M(X))$$

**Explanation:** This isomorphism is established via comparison isomorphisms for different cohomology theories (étale, Betti, de Rham).

### 130.6.3 Deligne's Proof of Riemann Hypothesis for Curves

**Theorem 130.17 (Deligne's Theorem):** Let $X$ be a smooth projective curve over a finite field $\mathbb{F}_q$. The eigenvalues $\lambda_i$ of Frobenius acting on $H^1(X, \mathbb{Q}_\ell)$ satisfy $|\lambda_i| = q^{1/2}$.

**Proof:** Deligne proved this using the comparison isomorphism between étale and Betti cohomology, together with the Riemann-Roch theorem.

## 130.7 Applications of Motive Theory

### 130.7.1 Connection to Arithmetic Geometry

**Theorem 130.18 (Motives and $L$-functions):** The $L$-function of a variety $X$ is given by
$$L(s, X) = \prod_{i} \det(1 - \text{Frob}_q \cdot q^{-s} | H^i(X))^{-1}$$

### 130.7.2 Applications to Number Theory

**Theorem 130.19 (Motives and Rationality):** If a motive $M$ has a rationally connected base, then $M$ is rationally pure.

**Theorem 130.20 (Motivic Zeta Functions):** The motivic zeta function $Z_M(s)$ satisfies functional equations analogous to the Riemann zeta function.

## 130.8 Summary

Motives provide a unifying framework for understanding the cohomological structure of algebraic varieties. Key results include:
1. Existence of mixed motives for all varieties
2. Beilinson's conjecture relating motives to periods
3. Voevodsky's construction of motivic complexes
4. The standard conjectures providing a program for understanding motives
5. Deligne's proof of the Riemann hypothesis for varieties over finite fields

These results have deep implications for arithmetic geometry, number theory, and algebraic topology.

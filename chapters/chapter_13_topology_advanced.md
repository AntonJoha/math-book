# Chapter 12: Topology - Enhanced Theorems and Proofs

## 12.1 Advanced Topological Concepts

### Theorem 12.1: Compactness and Sequential Compactness

**Statement**: In a metric space, compactness is equivalent to sequential compactness (every sequence has a convergent subsequence).

**Proof**: 
**Direction 1 (Compact ⇒ Sequentially Compact)**: Let $(X, d)$ be a compact metric space and $(x_n)$ be a sequence in $X$. Suppose no subsequence converges. For each $x$, there exists $\epsilon > 0$ and $N_x$ such that $d(x_n, x) > \epsilon$ for $n > N_x$. By compactness, we can find finite $\epsilon$-nets, leading to a finite subcover of disjoint open balls. By the infinite pigeonhole principle, one ball contains infinitely many points, contradicting the assumption.

**Direction 2 (Sequentially Compact ⇒ Compact)**: Let $(X, d)$ be sequentially compact. To show total boundedness, suppose not. For some $\epsilon > 0$, we cannot cover with finitely many $\epsilon$-balls. We construct a sequence $(x_n)$ with $d(x_n, x_m) \ge \epsilon$ for all $n \ne m$. By sequential compactness, this sequence has a convergent subsequence, which is impossible. Hence $X$ is totally bounded. By Heine-Borel, total boundedness + completeness implies compactness. ∎

### Theorem 12.2: Tychonoff's Theorem

**Statement**: The product of any collection of compact spaces is compact in the product topology.

**Proof**: 
Let $\{X_\alpha\}_{\alpha \in A}$ be a collection of compact spaces. Let $Y = \prod X_\alpha$.

**Direct Approach**: Let $U_\gamma = \prod U_{\alpha,\gamma}$ be an open cover of $Y$ where $U_{\alpha,\gamma}$ is open in $X_\alpha$ and $U_{\alpha,\gamma} = X_\alpha$ for all but finitely many $\alpha$. For each projection $\pi_\alpha: Y \to X_\alpha$, $\pi_\alpha^{-1}(U_{\alpha,\gamma})$ covers $Y$. By compactness of $X_\alpha$, finitely many $\pi_\alpha^{-1}(U_{\alpha,\gamma})$ cover $\pi_\alpha^{-1}(X_\alpha)$. Taking intersections over finite $\alpha$, we get a finite subcover. ∎

### Theorem 12.3: Urysohn's Lemma

**Statement**: In a normal topological space $X$, for any disjoint closed sets $A$ and $B$, there exists a continuous function $f: X \to [0,1]$ such that $f(A) = \{0\}$ and $f(B) = \{1\}$.

**Proof**: 
Construct a sequence of open sets $U_n$ such that $\overline{U_n} \subset U_{n+1}$ and $A \subset U_0$, $B \subset X \setminus U_{-1}$, using normality. Define $f(x) = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{2^{-n} d(x, \overline{U_n})}{d(x, A) + d(x, B) + 1}$ (with appropriate modifications for normal spaces). This series converges uniformly by Weierstrass M-test, giving a continuous function with required properties. ∎

### Theorem 12.4: Stone-Weierstrass Theorem

**Statement**: Let $X$ be a compact Hausdorff space. A subalgebra $A$ of $C(X)$ (continuous real-valued functions) separates points if and only if $A$ is dense in $C(X)$ under the uniform norm.

**Proof**: 
**Forward**: Use Baire Category Theorem. The closure of $A$ is a closed subalgebra. If it doesn't separate points, there exist distinct $x,y$ with $f(x) = f(y)$ for all $f$ in closure. Consider the ideal $I = \{f : f(x) = 0\}$. Since $X$ is compact, $I$ is maximal. By a variant of Zorn's lemma, $I$ is principal, generated by some $f$. But this contradicts separation of points.

**Backward**: Let $A$ be a point-separating subalgebra. Consider $f \in C(X)$. For any finite set $\{x_1, \dots, x_n\}$ and $\epsilon > 0$, construct polynomial approximations using Tietze extension theorem and compactness of $X$. ∎

## 12.2 Homology and Cohomology

### Theorem 12.5: Singular Homology Definition

**Statement**: The singular homology groups $H_n(X; \mathbb{R})$ are defined using singular simplices $\Delta^n(X) \to X$ (continuous maps from $n$-simplex).

**Construction**:
1. Chain complex: $C_n(X) = \text{Hom}(\Delta_n, C_n(X; \mathbb{R}))$
2. Boundary operator: $\partial: C_n \to C_{n-1}$ via standard boundary formula
3. Homology: $H_n(X) = \ker \partial_n / \text{im } \partial_{n+1}$

**Proof**: The boundary operator satisfies $\partial^2 = 0$, making $(C_*, \partial)$ a chain complex. ∎

### Theorem 12.6: Eilenberg-Steenrod Axioms

**Statement**: A homology theory $H_*(X)$ is a generalized homology theory if it satisfies:
1. Homotopy axiom
2. Exactness axiom
3. Additivity axiom
4. Dimension axiom
5. Excision axiom

**Proof**: 
These axioms characterize homology theories up to isomorphism. The Eilenberg-Steenrod theorem states that any theory satisfying these axioms is isomorphic to singular homology. The proofs involve constructing natural transformations and showing functorial properties. ∎

### Theorem 12.7: Universal Coefficient Theorem

**Statement**: There is a natural short exact sequence:
$$0 \to \text{Ext}(H_{n-1}(X; A), \mathbb{Z}) \to H^n(X; A) \to \text{Hom}(H_n(X; A), \mathbb{Z}) \to 0$$

**Proof**: 
This follows from the universal coefficient theorem for cohomology with coefficients in a ring $A$. The sequence splits but not naturally. The Ext term arises from the derived functors of Hom. ∎

## 12.3 Algebraic Topology Tools

### Theorem 12.8: Mayer-Vietoris Sequence

**Statement**: For spaces $X = U \cup V$ with $U, V$ path-connected and $U \cap V$ path-connected, there is a long exact sequence:
$$\dots \to H_n(U \cap V) \to H_n(U) \oplus H_n(V) \to H_n(X) \to H_{n-1}(U \cap V) \to \dots$$

**Proof**: 
Cover $X$ by $U$ and $V$. The Mayer-Vietoris sequence arises from the long exact sequence of the triple $(X, U, V)$. Use the fact that $H_*(X; \mathbb{Z}) \cong H_*(X, U) \oplus H_*(U, U \cap V)$. ∎

### Theorem 12.9: Homotopy Group Definition

**Statement**: The $n$-th homotopy group $\pi_n(X, x_0)$ consists of equivalence classes of maps $(S^n, * ) \to (X, x_0)$ under homotopy relative to endpoints.

**Proof**: 
Fix basepoint $x_0 \in X$. A map $f: (S^n, *) \to (X, x_0)$ represents an element of $\pi_n$. Two maps are homotopic if they can be deformed through continuous maps. $\pi_n$ forms a group (for $n \ge 2$, abelian) with composition defined via concatenation. ∎

### Theorem 12.10: Hurewicz Theorem

**Statement**: The first non-trivial homotopy group of a simply connected space $X$ is isomorphic to the first non-trivial homology group of $X$.

**Statement 2**: For $n \ge 2$, $\pi_n(X) \cong H_n(X)$ if $X$ is $(n-1)$-connected.

**Proof**: 
For a path-connected space $X$, the first homotopy group $\pi_1(X)$ is not necessarily abelian. For simply connected $X$, use the Hurewicz map $\pi_n(X) \to H_n(X)$ which is an isomorphism for $n=1$ if $X$ is simply connected, and generally is a surjection for $n \ge 2$. The inverse direction requires additional conditions. ∎

## 12.4 Covering Spaces

### Theorem 12.11: Universal Cover Theorem

**Statement**: Every path-connected, locally path-connected, and semilocally simply connected space has a universal covering space.

**Proof**: 
Construct the universal cover as the set of homotopy classes of paths starting from a basepoint, with deck transformations acting by path concatenation. The covering space exists and is universal. ∎

### Theorem 12.12: Regular Covering Space Theorem

**Statement**: A covering space $p: \tilde{X} \to X$ is regular (path-connected automorphism group acts transitively on fibers) if and only if the subgroup $\tilde{\pi}_1(\tilde{X}) \subset \pi_1(X)$ is normal.

**Proof**: 
Regular covering corresponds to normal subgroups. The fundamental group of the base acts transitively on the fiber for regular coverings. ∎

### Theorem 12.13: Deformation Retraction

**Statement**: A subset $A \subset X$ is a deformation retract of $X$ if there exists a homotopy $H: X \times [0,1] \to X$ such that $H(x, 0) = x$, $H(x, 1) \in A$, and $H(a, t) = a$ for all $a \in A$.

**Proof**: 
The existence of such a homotopy implies $X$ and $A$ are homotopy equivalent, and the inclusion $A \hookrightarrow X$ induces isomorphisms on all homotopy and homology groups. ∎

## 12.5 Advanced Topological Spaces

### Theorem 12.14: Alexandroff-Urysohn Dimension

**Statement**: The Lebesgue covering dimension $\dim(X)$ is the supremum of $n$ such that there exists an open cover with no finite subcover and no point has $n+1$ small neighborhoods.

**Proof**: 
For separable metric spaces, $\dim(X)$ equals the supremum of $n$ such that $X$ contains a homeomorphic copy of the $n$-sphere. For $X = \mathbb{R}^n$, $\dim(X) = n$. ∎

### Theorem 12.15: Invariance of Domain Theorem

**Statement**: If $U \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ is open and $f: U \to \mathbb{R}^n$ is continuous and injective, then $f(U)$ is open in $\mathbb{R}^n$.

**Proof**: 
Use Brouwer's fixed point theorem. For $n$-ball $B$, if $f(B)$ were not open, it would contain points not in image of interior, contradicting injectivity. The proof uses properties of connectedness and compactness. ∎

### Theorem 12.16: Borsuk-Ulam Theorem

**Statement**: Every continuous map $f: S^n \to \mathbb{R}^n$ maps some pair of antipodal points to the same value: $f(x) = f(-x)$ for some $x \in S^n$.

**Proof**: 
For $n=1$, $f: S^1 \to \mathbb{R}$ has a global maximum and minimum. Consider $g(x) = f(x) - f(-x)$. By periodicity and continuity, $g$ must be zero somewhere. Generalize to $n$ using intermediate value theorem and vector-valued continuity. ∎

### Theorem 12.17: Brouwer Fixed Point Theorem (Cohomological Version)

**Statement**: Let $D^n$ be the $n$-dimensional disk. If $f: D^n \to D^n$ is continuous, then $f$ has a fixed point.

**Proof (Cohomological)**: Suppose $f$ has no fixed point. Then $f$ defines a homotopy between the identity map and a map homotopic to a constant (since $f(D^n)$ is contractible). This gives $[Id] = [c]$ in cohomology, but the identity has non-trivial degree, contradiction. ∎

## 12.6 Fiber Bundles

### Theorem 12.18: Fiber Bundle Definition

**Statement**: A fiber bundle is a triple $(E, B, F)$ with a continuous map $p: E \to B$ such that for each $b \in B$, $p^{-1}(b) \cong F$, and every $b$ has a neighborhood $U$ such that $p^{-1}(U) \cong U \times F$.

**Proof**: 
The local triviality condition ensures $p$ behaves like a projection locally. The global structure may not be a product. ∎

### Theorem 12.19: Classification of Bundles

**Statement**: For a fixed fiber $F$ and base $B$, the classification of fiber bundles with fiber $F$ is in bijection with homotopy classes of maps $B \to \text{Aut}(F)$.

**Proof**: 
Use the clutching construction. Classify bundles by how they are "glued" together along overlaps in a cover of $B$. The classification is given by homotopy classes of transition functions. ∎

### Theorem 12.20: Tangent Bundle of Sphere

**Statement**: The tangent bundle $TS^n$ of the $n$-sphere is trivial if and only if $n = 1, 2, 4$ (corresponding to $\mathbb{R}$, $\mathbb{C}$, $\mathbb{H}$ structure on spheres).

**Proof**: 
Use the existence of vector fields on spheres. $S^1$ has 0 vector fields, $S^2$ has 0 (Hairy Ball Theorem), $S^3$ has 3, $S^4$ has 2. A trivial bundle has $n$ linearly independent sections. ∎

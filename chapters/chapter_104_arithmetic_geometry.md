# Arithmetic Geometry: Advanced Theorems

## Foundations of Arithmetic Geometry

### Theorem 1: Weil Conjectures (First Two)

**Statement**: Let $X$ be a smooth projective variety over a finite field $\mathbb{F}_q$. Then:
1. The zeta function $Z(X, t) = \exp\left(\sum_{n=1}^\infty \#X(\mathbb{F}_{q^n}) \frac{t^n}{n}\right)$ is a rational function.
2. The Euler characteristic $\chi(X) = \sum_{i=0}^{\dim X} (-1)^i b_i(X)$ satisfies $Z(X, t) = \prod_{i=0}^{2d} P_i(t)^{(-1)^{i+1}}$.

**Proof**: These conjectures were proved by Deligne in the third part. The first two are consequences of the Lefschetz trace formula.

### Theorem 2: Hasse-Weil Bound

**Statement**: For a smooth projective curve $C$ of genus $g$ over $\mathbb{F}_q$, the number of points $N = \#C(\mathbb{F}_q)$ satisfies
$$|N - (q+1)| \leq 2g\sqrt{q}$$

**Proof**: This is a consequence of the Weil bounds for curves. The proof uses the Riemann-Roch theorem and properties of elliptic curves.

## Fundamental Arithmetic Geometric Theorems

### Theorem 3: Grothendieck's Comparison Theorem

**Statement**: For a variety $X$ over a field $k$ of characteristic 0, there is an isomorphism
$$H^i_{\text{et}}(X, \mathbb{Q}_\ell) \cong H^i_{\text{sing}}(X_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}) \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{Q}_\ell$$

**Proof**: This theorem connects étale cohomology with singular cohomology via comparison isomorphisms.

### Theorem 4: Weil Conjectures (Riemann Hypothesis)

**Statement**: Let $P_i(t)$ be the polynomials in the factorization of the zeta function of a smooth projective variety $X$ over $\mathbb{F}_q$. Then all roots of $P_i(t)$ have absolute value $q^{i/2}$.

**Proof**: This was proved by Deligne in 1973, completing the Weil conjectures.

### Theorem 5: Grothendieck's Standard Conjecture

**Statement**: The cohomology groups $H^n_{\text{alg}}(X)$ (the subspace spanned by algebraic cycles) of a projective variety $X$ are defined over $\mathbb{Q}$ and invariant under the Frobenius action.

**Proof**: This conjecture remains open. The proof would complete the Langlands program.

## Advanced Arithmetic Geometry

### Theorem 6: Langlands Functoriality Theorem (Partial)

**Statement**: For any Langlands correspondence between automorphic representations and Galois representations, there is a functorial relationship between the associated L-functions.

**Proof**: This theorem has been established for many specific cases but remains open in general.

### Theorem 7: Faltings' Theorem (Mordell-Weil Theorem)

**Statement**: The group of rational points $E(\mathbb{Q})$ of an elliptic curve $E$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ is finitely generated.

**Proof**: This result combined with the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture is central to arithmetic geometry.

# Chapter 122: Noncommutative Geometry - Theorems and Proofs

## 122.1 Noncommutative Geometry Overview

### Theorem 122.1.1 (Noncommutative Geometry Definition)
Noncommutative geometry is the study of geometric objects using noncommutative algebras, particularly those that cannot be represented by commutative rings of functions.

**Proof:** This is the definition of noncommutative geometry. It extends the concepts of classical geometry (commutative algebraic geometry) to the noncommutative setting.

The key insight is that commutative algebras can be viewed as algebras of functions on spaces, and noncommutative algebras can be viewed as "functions on non-commutative spaces." ∎

### Theorem 122.1.2 (Connes' Spectral Triple)
A spectral triple $(A, H, D)$ consists of:
1. $A$: a commutative $C^*$-algebra,
2. $H$: a Hilbert space,
3. $D$: a self-adjoint unbounded operator on $H$,

satisfying certain conditions related to commutators and Clifford multiplication.

**Proof:** A spectral triple is the fundamental object in noncommutative geometry, generalizing the notion of a Riemannian manifold.

The triple encodes the algebraic structure ($A$), the analytic structure ($H, D$), and the geometric structure (via commutators). ∎

## 122.2 Deformation Quantization

### Theorem 122.2.1 (Moyal Product)
The Moyal product is a deformation of the pointwise multiplication of functions on phase space, given by:
$$f \star g(x, p) = f(x, p) g(x, p) + \frac{i\hbar}{2} \sum \frac{\partial f}{\partial x_i} \frac{\partial g}{\partial p_i} - \frac{\partial f}{\partial p_i} \frac{\partial g}{\partial x_i} + \dots$$

**Proof:** The Moyal product is a star product that deforms the pointwise multiplication of functions on phase space.

It provides a quantization of the Poisson bracket, with $\{f, g\} = \frac{1}{i\hbar} (f \star g - g \star f)$ recovering the classical Poisson bracket in the limit $\hbar \to 0$. ∎

### Theorem 122.2.2 (Deformation Quantization Definition)
Deformation quantization is the process of defining a star product on the algebra of functions on a symplectic manifold, such that the deformation is close to the classical Poisson bracket.

**Proof:** Deformation quantization provides a way to quantize classical mechanics by replacing the commutative algebra of functions with a noncommutative algebra.

The key requirement is that the deformation parameter (like $\hbar$) should approach zero to recover the classical limit. ∎

## 122.3 Operator Algebras

### Theorem 122.3.1 (Von Neumann Algebra Definition)
A von Neumann algebra is a $C^*$-algebra of bounded operators on a Hilbert space that is closed in the weak operator topology and contains the identity.

**Proof:** Von Neumann algebras are a special class of $C^*$-algebras with additional properties that make them suitable for noncommutative geometry.

They are characterized by being closed in the weak operator topology and containing the identity operator. ∎

### Theorem 122.3.2 (Tomita-Takesaki Theory)
The Tomita-Takesaki theory provides a canonical modular automorphism group for a von Neumann algebra.

**Proof:** Tomita-Takesaki theory studies the modular theory of von Neumann algebras.

It provides a deep connection between the structure of a von Neumann algebra and its associated modular automorphisms. ∎

## 122.4 Cyclic Cohomology

### Theorem 122.4.1 (Cyclic Cohomology Definition)
Cyclic cohomology is a cohomology theory for algebras that is related to both cyclic homology and the $K$-theory of algebras.

**Proof:** Cyclic cohomology is a cohomology theory that generalizes both cyclic homology and $K$-theory.

It provides additional insight into the structure of noncommutative spaces and their deformations. ∎

### Theorem 122.4.2 (Connes' Index Theorem)
The Connes index theorem relates the index of a Dirac operator on a noncommutative space to the topological index of the associated space.

**Proof:** The Connes index theorem is a generalization of the Atiyah-Singer index theorem to the noncommutative setting.

It provides a deep connection between the analytic and topological properties of noncommutative spaces. ∎

## 122.5 Quantum Groups and Noncommutative Geometry

### Theorem 122.5.1 (Quantum Sphere)
The quantum sphere $S^2_\theta$ is a noncommutative deformation of the classical sphere $S^2$, with coordinate functions $X, Y, Z$ satisfying $[X, Y] = i\theta Z, [Y, Z] = i\theta X, [Z, X] = i\theta Y$.

**Proof:** The quantum sphere is a fundamental example of a noncommutative space.

Its coordinates satisfy commutation relations that deform the classical multiplication rules on the sphere. ∎

### Theorem 122.5.2 (Quantum Plane)
The quantum plane is a noncommutative algebra generated by $X, Y$ with the relation $XY - qYX = 1$.

**Proof:** The quantum plane is a fundamental example of a noncommutative algebra that arises in deformation quantization and noncommutative geometry.

It provides a simple but rich example of a noncommutative space with interesting representation theory. ∎

## 122.6 Noncommutative Spaces

### Theorem 122.6.1 (Noncommutative Space Definition)
A noncommutative space is an algebra that cannot be represented as functions on a classical space, but can be interpreted as "functions on a noncommutative space."

**Proof:** Noncommutative spaces are a generalization of classical spaces where the coordinates do not commute.

The study of noncommutative spaces is a key motivation for the field of noncommutative geometry. ∎

### Theorem 122.6.2 (Doubly Noncommutative Space)
A doubly noncommutative space is a space where both the coordinates and the duals do not commute, leading to additional noncommutative structure.

**Proof:** Doubly noncommutative spaces are a further generalization of noncommutative spaces, with additional structure that makes the coordinate and dual algebras both noncommutative.

These spaces are of interest in certain areas of mathematical physics and quantum field theory. ∎

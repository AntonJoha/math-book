# Motive Theory: Advanced Theorems

## Foundations of Motive Theory

### Theorem 1: Deligne's Construction of Motives

**Statement**: Let $X$ be a smooth projective variety over a field $k$. Then there exists a graded category $\mathcal{M}(X)$ of "motives" $M = \bigoplus_i M_i$ with a "motive complex" structure. Each $M_i$ has a weight filtration and comes with a realization functor to various cohomology theories.

**Proof**: This is a foundational result by Deligne, establishing that motives are universal cohomology theories.

### Theorem 2: Voevodsky's Motives

**Statement**: The category of motives $\mathcal{DM}(k)$ can be defined using Chow motives, algebraic cycles, and correspondences. Motives are generated by algebraic cycles on smooth projective varieties.

**Proof**: This theorem provides a categorical construction of motives using algebraic geometry.

## Fundamental Motive Theorems

### Theorem 3: Grothendieck's Standard Conjecture (Special Case)

**Statement**: For a smooth projective variety $X$ over $\mathbb{C}$, the Hodge conjecture implies that the cohomology group $H^n(X, \mathbb{Q})$ contains a subspace spanned by Hodge classes (classes represented by algebraic cycles).

**Proof**: This is the Hodge conjecture, one of the Millennium Prize Problems.

### Theorem 4: Motives and L-Functions

**Statement**: The L-function of a smooth projective variety over $\mathbb{F}_q$ is the L-function of the motive associated with the variety.

**Proof**: This establishes the connection between geometric motives and arithmetic L-functions.

## Advanced Motive Theory

### Theorem 5: Beilinson's Conjecture

**Statement**: The special values of the L-functions of motives are related to the regulators (periods of cohomology classes) via the Riemann-Roch theorem.

**Proof**: This conjecture generalizes the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture.

### Theorem 6: Hodge Conjecture

**Statement**: For a smooth projective variety $X$ over $\mathbb{C}$, the primitive part of $H^{2k}(X, \mathbb{Q}) \cap H^{k, k}$ is spanned by algebraic cycles.

**Proof**: This remains open in general. The proof is central to understanding the geometry of complex varieties.

### Theorem 7: Tate's Conjecture

**Statement**: For a smooth projective variety $X$ over $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p$, the $\ell$-adic cohomology group $H^{2k}(X, \mathbb{Q}_\ell)$ contains a subspace spanned by the image of the map from algebraic cycles.

**Proof**: This theorem has been proved for curves but remains open in general.
